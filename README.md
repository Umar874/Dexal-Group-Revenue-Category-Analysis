# Dexal-Group-Revenue-Category-Analysis
A Power BI dashboard analyzing $435K in 2023 grocery sales for Dexal Group of Companies, covering monthly revenue trend, product performance, category mix, and regional distribution. Includes a full technical report with a data-backed breakdown of seasonal patterns and category concentration risk.

Revenue peaked twice in 2023, once in June, once in December, and both peaks were followed by an immediate pullback the very next month. That rhythm is the backbone of this entire analysis.

**Overview**

I analyzed $435,070 in 2023 grocery retail sales for Dexal Group of Companies, tracking revenue by month, product, category, region, salesperson, and customer. The result is a Power BI dashboard built around five live KPIs and seven core visualizations, focused specifically on seasonal trend, product concentration, and category mix.

**The Problem**

A yearly revenue total flattens twelve very different months into one number. This business's $435K year looked steady on the surface, but breaking it down by month revealed a clear, repeatable rhythm, two sharp peaks, two predictable dips, that a single annual figure would never show. The same was true of the product and category mix, a handful of items and one dominant category were carrying far more weight than an even distribution would suggest.

The dashboard combines Sum of Revenue, Max Quantity, Sum of Quantity, Total Cities, and Max Unit Price KPI cards with a monthly revenue trend line, salesperson performance, a state-level revenue map, product performance, customer performance, category mix, and regional revenue distribution.

**What I Found**

Revenue opened the year at 33K in January, dipped to a first-half low of 20K in February, climbed steadily to a mid-year peak of 56K in June, dropped sharply to 27K in July, then closed the year at its highest point of all, 67K in December. That is not a random walk through twelve months. It is a two-peak pattern specific enough to plan staffing and inventory around, and a dip pattern (February, and a smaller one in November) specific enough to target directly with promotions.

Product performance told its own concentration story. Coffee led at 75K, followed closely by Curry at 69K. Together, those two products outproduced the six lowest-performing items on the chart combined. Category data reinforced the same pattern at a higher level, Beverages alone accounted for 47.84% of total revenue, close to half the business riding on a single category.

Regional performance stayed consistent with what a related analysis on this same dataset had already shown. North led every other region at 141.68K, well ahead of West's 91.25K, a gap wide enough to represent a real strategic imbalance rather than statistical noise.

**Why It Matters**

None of these patterns needed new data. They needed the existing numbers cut a different way, month against month, product against product, category against category, until the seasonal rhythm and concentration risk sitting underneath a single annual total became impossible to miss.
